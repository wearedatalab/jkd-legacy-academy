Awareness is the first interception

Recognise danger before it happens

If you spot a problem early, you have more options. We use four simple awareness levels. Tap each one to see what it means.

Calmly observing your surroundings, without anxiety. This is where we want you most of the time.

These are teaching categories, not a prediction system. The goal is relaxed awareness, not permanent hypervigilance.

What to look for

  • Someone repeatedly changing direction to stay close to you.
  • A person moving into your personal space.
  • Persistence after a clear “no”.
  • Someone trying to isolate you from other people.
  • Unusual attention to your phone, handbag or movements.
  • Blocked exits or poorly lit areas.
  • A distraction in front of you while someone else approaches.

Appearance, clothing, ethnicity or background don’t make someone dangerous. We focus on observable behaviour and context.

Psychology of confrontation

Your voice is a self-defence tool

Many dangerous encounters start with pressure, intimidation or a boundary being ignored, not with violence. You’ll learn why people freeze, how the compliance trap works, and four short phrases you can say clearly under stress.

  • Someone comes too close“Please step back.”
  • They keep pushing after you say no“No. I’m not interested.”
  • Someone tries to engage aggressively“I don’t want any trouble.”
  • Immediate threat“Stay back! Don’t come closer!”

You don’t owe anyone an explanation for protecting your personal space.

The signature module

What’s in your handbag that could help protect you?

Your handbag is not an arsenal. It’s a collection of resources that can help you create distance, attract attention, get help and escape. Bring yours on the day: we work with what you really carry.

  • Mobile phoneCall 000, alert your emergency contacts, share your location and use the torch to see and be seen.
  • HandbagHold it securely and use it as a temporary barrier to keep space. Never risk injury to protect possessions.
  • UmbrellaCreates visual and physical separation while you move towards an exit.
  • Water bottleKeep it accessible: a temporary barrier to help you create space.
  • Keys & small torchKeys get you into a safe place; a torch helps you find exits and stay visible.

Other useful items: a whistle, a personal alarm and a charged power bank. We don’t teach everyday objects as weapons, we don’t recommend holding keys between your fingers (it can injure your own hand), and we explain calling 000 and acting reasonably and proportionately under Australian law.

Two hours, six modules

More time on prevention than on fighting. A small number of simple skills, practised until they stick.

  1. 0:00

    Module 1The JKD mindset

    Self-defence begins before physical contact. Prevention, preparation and protection, and why the goal is to get home safely, not to win a fight.

  2. 0:10

    Module 2Situational awareness

    The four awareness levels, the behaviours that matter and a hands-on observation challenge: what did you notice, and where were the exits?

  3. 0:30

    Module 3Psychology & verbal de-escalation

    The freeze response, the compliance trap and personal boundaries. Practise voice, posture and distance in pairs.

  4. 1:00

    Module 4The handbag module

    What’s in your handbag? Three practical stations: the handbag as a barrier, the umbrella for space, the phone and alarm for help.

  5. 1:25

    Module 5Simple, direct physical skills

    Protective posture, distance management, moving away from a grab, a controlled pad drill to create your chance to escape, and getting up safely from the ground.

  6. 1:50

    Module 6Real-world scenarios

    Three short, supervised scenarios run slowly first: a stranger who ignores your boundary, being followed to a car park, a handbag grab.

FAQ

Before you register

Do I need martial arts experience or to be fit?

No. The workshop is designed for everyday women, not athletes or martial artists. Every exercise starts from zero and has gentler options, so you can take part at your own level.

How much does it cost?

The early-bird price is A$99 for the first four places. The remaining eight places are A$120. There are only 12 places in total, to keep the training practical and personal.

How do I pay?

Reserve your place with the form. We’ll confirm whether an early-bird or standard place is available and send you the payment details. Your place is confirmed once payment is received, and we then send you the joining instructions.

What should I wear and bring?

Comfortable clothes you can move in. Bring your handbag and the everyday items you normally carry: we use them in the handbag module. A water bottle is a good idea too.

Is there physical contact or sparring?

There is no sparring. Partner exercises are slow and supervised, with consent before each one, and you can opt out of physical contact at any time. Pad work is done on focus pads, never on a partner.

I have been through a difficult experience. Is this for me?

You are very welcome. The environment is supportive and calm: you will never be asked to share personal experiences, there are no surprise or frightening simulations, and you can step out of any exercise.

Can I bring a friend or family member?

Yes, and we encourage it. Practising with someone you know makes the drills more fun and helps you keep the awareness habits going afterwards. Tell us how many places you need in the form.

Where is it and where do I park?

At Jeet Kune Do Legacy Academy, Unit 6 / 72-80 Hampstead Rd, Maidstone VIC 3012, in Melbourne’s inner west. There is free parking on site and bus 406 stops nearby.

Who teaches the workshop?

Sigung Ricardo Vargas, head instructor of Jeet Kune Do Legacy Academy: a second-generation Jeet Kune Do instructor certified by Sifu Jerry Poteet and Sigung Richard Bustillo, both direct students of Bruce Lee.
